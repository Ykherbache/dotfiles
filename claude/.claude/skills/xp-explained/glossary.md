# Glossary — XP Explained

**Accepted Responsibility** — Responsibility can only be accepted, never assigned. With responsibility comes authority; misalignment of the two distorts communication.

**Baby Steps** — Make the smallest recognizable change in the right direction. Overhead of small steps is less than recoiling from aborted big changes.

**Corollary Practices** — Practices that are difficult or dangerous without the primary practices in place (e.g., daily deployment without low defect rates is a disaster).

**Conquer and Divide** — Start with a small team solving a small problem, then split along natural fracture lines. Opposite of divide-and-conquer.

**Continuous Integration** — Integrate and test changes after no more than a couple of hours. Prefer synchronous (wait for build) over asynchronous (email notification).

**Defect Cost Increase (DCI)** — Empirically verified: the sooner you find a defect, the cheaper it is to fix. XP uses DCI in reverse by testing in the inner loop of programming.

**Double-Checking** — State what a computation does twice in two different ways (test and implementation). If they agree, likely correct.

**Driving Metaphor** — Development is not pointing straight down the road; it's constantly paying attention and making small corrections.

**Energized Work** — Work only sustainable hours. Insight comes to prepared, rested, relaxed minds. Long hours are a control illusion.

**Five Whys** — Taiichi Ohno's technique: ask "why" five times to find the people problem at the root of any defect.

**Flow** — Deliver value in a steady stream rather than big chunks. Smaller batches = less risk, more feedback.

**Incremental Design** — Design every day in light of experience. "Design always" — not BDUF, not no design. Eliminate duplication as the primary design heuristic.

**Informative Workspace** — An observer gets project status in 15 seconds. Stories on a wall, big visible charts.

**Kaizen** — Toyota's continuous improvement. Workers identify waste, analyze, experiment, standardize results.

**Mentality of Sufficiency** — Act as if you have enough time, money, and skills. Fussing about constraints distracts from goals.

**Mutual Benefit** — Every activity should benefit me now, me later, and my customer. The most important and hardest XP principle. Win-win-win.

**Once and Only Once** — Data, structure, or logic should exist in only one place. The most powerful design heuristic. One conceptual change = one code change.

**Pair Programming** — Two people, one machine, programming as dialogue. Rotate pairs frequently. Not required 100% of the time; honor the need for solitude.

**Primary Practices** — Practices safe to adopt independently, giving immediate improvement regardless of other practices.

**Pull Model** — Stories specified just before implementation; tests pulled from specs; code written to match tests. Contrast with push model (piling up requirements).

**Quarterly Cycle** — Plan themes, identify bottlenecks, align with organizational goals. A season-length heartbeat.

**Scope (as control variable)** — Time and cost are usually fixed. Quality shouldn't be sacrificed. Scope is the lever: make it explicit, negotiate it.

**Self-Similarity** — Copy structures that work at one scale to another. The test→code→refactor rhythm works at hours, weeks, and quarters.

**Shrinking Teams** — As capability grows, keep workload constant but reduce team size. Freed people form new teams. (Toyota practice.)

**Slack** — Include droppable low-priority tasks in every plan. Meeting commitments rebuilds trust. "If you get stuck in two-wheel-drive, you have four-wheel-drive to get out."

**Stories** — Units of customer-visible functionality. Not "requirements" (which implies absolutism). Estimated early. Written on index cards.

**Ten-Minute Build** — Automated build + all tests in ≤10 minutes. Longer builds get used less often, killing feedback.

**Theory of Constraints** — In any system, one constraint at a time limits throughput. Find it (work piles up before it), optimize it, then find the next.

**Vulnerability is Safety** — Holding back effort doesn't protect you from failure. Doing your best and being open about it is the true safety.

**Weekly Cycle** — Plan Monday, write tests for stories, implement, deploy by Friday. The week is a natural, shared timescale.

**Whole Team** — Cross-functional team with all skills needed. People identified with the team, not their function. Ideal sizes: ≤12 for close interaction, ≤150 for face recognition.

**Yesterday's Weather** — Plan this week for exactly as much work as you accomplished last week. Simple, honest capacity estimation.
