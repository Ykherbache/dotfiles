# Chapter 4 — Values

The five values are the roots of everything in XP. The biggest problem in software development is focus on individual action rather than team behavior.

## Communication
Most problems have a known solution — the knowledge just isn't reaching the right person. Ask: was this problem caused by a lack of communication? What communication do you need now? What will keep you out of this trouble in the future?

## Simplicity
"What is the simplest thing that could possibly work?" — not "too simple to work." Bias toward eliminating wasted complexity. Simplicity only makes sense in context (parser generators are simple if the team knows them).

## Feedback
No fixed direction remains valid for long. Three reasons you can't "just do it right the first time": (1) you may not know how, (2) what's right today may be wrong tomorrow, (3) doing everything right may take too long. Shorten feedback to minutes/hours. It's possible to get too much feedback — slow down if you're ignoring it.

## Courage
Effective action in the face of fear. Sometimes bias to action; sometimes patience to wait for the real problem to emerge. Courage alone is dangerous — counterbalance with other values.

## Respect
Below the surface of the other four. Every person whose life is touched by software development has equal value. If members don't care about each other, XP won't work.

## Others
Your team may hold additional values (safety, security, predictability, quality-of-life). What matters most is aligning team behavior to team values.

## Actionable Takeaway
Style debates (coding conventions, tools) matter less than the team choosing to work toward a common standard. Focus on team behavior, not individual preferences.
