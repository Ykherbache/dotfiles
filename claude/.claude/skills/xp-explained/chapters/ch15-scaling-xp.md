# Chapter 15 — Scaling XP

XP scales along seven dimensions, not just team size.

## Seven Dimensions

**Number of People** — First ask: can a smaller team solve this? If not, start small, conquer, then divide along natural fracture lines. Integrate frequently across teams. "Conquer-and-divide, not divide-and-conquer."

**Investment** — XP can be expensed (ongoing maintenance) or capitalized (quarterly-approved projects). Find an ally in finance early.

**Organization Size** — Maintain communication with the rest of the org in forms they expect. A skilled project manager translates cards-on-wall into slide formats. Don't push your newfound power on others. One team satisfied quarterly plan requirements by having the PM record what was done each Friday — most precise estimates in the org.

**Time** — Long-running projects work well because tests prevent maintenance mistakes. For start-stop projects, write a "Rosetta Stone" document before shutdown: how to build/test + interesting starting places.

**Problem Complexity** — XP suits projects requiring close specialist cooperation. Pair with specialists — you won't become one, but you'll catch mistakes and strengthen the team.

**Solution Complexity** — For overly complex systems, stop making it worse first. Get build under control, institute stories and story board, then chip away at excess complexity while continuing to deliver. One team: 70→20 engineers, 66% faster defect fixes, 75% faster releases.

**Consequences of Failure** — Safety/security-critical: the #1 value becomes safety. Traceability is built into XP (story → system test → unit test → code change). Auditing should happen early and often, not as an end-phase. Build relationship with your auditor.

## Key Insight
With awareness and appropriate adaptations, XP does scale. The values and principles apply at all scales; the practices can be modified.
