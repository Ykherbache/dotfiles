# Chapter 8 — Getting Started

You've already started. XP improves both your process and your experience of it. There is no one right place to begin.

## How to Start
1. Pick the primary practice whose purpose matches your biggest opportunity for improvement
2. Move one step closer to the practice's endpoint
3. Evaluate whether it helped
4. Pick the next practice

You can apply XP-style planning to your own improvement: write stories ("Automate the build", "Pair with Joe for 2 hours"), estimate, set a budget, pick the first one.

## Pace of Change
Eager teams can progress quickly. If you change too fast, you risk slipping back — regroup, review values, remind yourself why you chose these practices. New habits take time to solidify.

## Change Starts at Home
The only person you can actually change is yourself. Don't dictate practices — raise expectations for accountability and teamwork, then help the team through the anxiety of change.

## Mapping Exercise
For each practice: draw the practice in the center, attach factors that affect it, symptoms that it isn't going well. Discuss as a team. Post the maps. When one change is in place, look for what new changes have come into reach.

## Key Insight
"If there is one message: software development is capable of much, much more than it is currently delivering." Defects should be rare. Major scope adjustments should occur only in the first half of schedules. Initial deployment should come after a small percentage of the budget is spent.
