# Chapter 16 — Interview (Sabre Airline Solutions)

Case study: Brad Jensen, SVP at Sabre. 300 people (240 developers), 13 product teams unified under one architecture.

## Key Findings

- **Training**: 13 groups × 1 week each. In hindsight, would start with one team, let them master it, then teach the next.
- **Not all-or-nothing**: "I have a dial in my head." Pure XP for greenfield Java; XP embedded in waterfall for legacy C++.
- **Results**: One pure XP project — zero defects in 2 years. Legacy projects: 1-2 defects/KLOC vs industry 8/KLOC. 40% productivity increase on one comparable project.
- **Adoption curve**: 1/3 skeptical, 1/3 buy in quickly, 1/3 wait and see. Eventually 80-90% buy in. "If programmers won't pair or insist on owning code, have the courage to fire them."
- **On-site customers**: Most valuable part of XP but causes most problems. Good customers write stories and acceptance criteria. Bad customers conceal requirements to game estimates.
- **Scope management**: "Great to know a quarter of the way through whether you'll make it. But without careful watching, scope management can turn into scope creep."

## Executive Advice
"Definitely use XP. Plan by features customers care about. Plan releases quarterly. Make iterations absolutely fixed. Have customers sit with the team. Put the team in an open space."
