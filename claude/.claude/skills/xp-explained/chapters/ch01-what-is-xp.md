# Chapter 1 — What is XP?

XP is about social change — letting go of habits that were adaptive in the past but now interfere with best work. It reconciles humanity and productivity: the more humanely you treat yourself and others, the more productive everyone becomes.

## Key Formulations

- "XP is giving up old, ineffective technical and social habits in favor of new ones that work"
- "Vulnerability is safety" — holding back 20% doesn't protect you; doing your best and accepting consequences is the real safety
- "Mentality of sufficiency" — act as if you have enough time, money, skills; fussing about constraints distracts from goals
- "It's not my job to manage someone else's expectations. It's their job. It's my job to do my best and communicate clearly"

## XP Addresses Risk

| Risk | XP Response |
|---|---|
| Schedule slips | Short cycles, weekly iterations, highest-priority first |
| Project canceled | Smallest valuable release first |
| System goes sour | Comprehensive automated tests, always deployable |
| Defect rate | Tests from both programmer and customer perspectives |
| Business misunderstood | Business people as first-class team members |
| Staff turnover | Programmers estimate own work, human contact, gradual onboarding |

## XP Consists Of

1. A philosophy based on five values
2. A body of practices proven useful
3. Complementary principles for bridging values to practices
4. A community sharing these values

## Actionable Takeaway

When facing a dilemma, ask: "How would I do this if I had enough time?" Your clear self does the best work regardless of constraints.
